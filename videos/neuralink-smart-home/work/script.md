[beat: evidence 1 — N3 chip specs]

Now here's what changed everything. Neuralink's next-generation N3 implant doesn't just read brain signals — it processes them on-device. The chip has a custom ASIC with spiking neural network cores and a lightweight transformer accelerator. It runs its AI model locally — processing your thoughts in real time — in under fifty milliseconds, within a one-milliwatt power envelope.

Translation: the AI that decodes your thoughts lives inside your skull. It doesn't need the cloud. It doesn't need Wi-Fi. It doesn't need a hub sitting on your shelf collecting dust. That fifty-millisecond number means your house responds before your mouth finishes saying "turn on the lights."

[beat: evidence 2 — latency comparison]

Compare that to your current smart home setup. You say "Alexa, turn on the lights." The wake word travels to the speaker. The speaker sends it to the cloud. The cloud processes it. The response travels back. Total round trip: five hundred milliseconds to two seconds. Sometimes longer. Sometimes it doesn't hear you at all.

Neuralink's neural interface cuts that to under fifty milliseconds. That's the time it takes to blink. Your house responds before your conscious mind finishes forming the thought.

[beat: evidence 3 — Samsung partnership]

And this isn't happening in isolation. Neuralink announced a partnership with Samsung earlier this year. Samsung makes the chips. Neuralink designs the neural interface. Together, they're building the manufacturing infrastructure to scale this from twenty-one patients to millions.

The Austin factory is already producing N1 implants at volume. The R1 surgical robot performs the implantation with precision no human surgeon can match — threading electrodes into the cortex while avoiding blood vessels, all in a procedure Neuralink wants to make as routine as LASIK.

[beat: evidence 4 — smart home integration]

Here's where it gets personal for anyone with a smart home. Neuralink's platform already supports direct API integration with external devices. Patients are controlling wheelchairs, robotic arms, and yes — smart home systems — through thought alone.

But the current system still routes through a smartphone as an intermediary. The N3 removes that step entirely. Direct brain-to-device communication. No phone. No hub. No voice assistant processing your commands through a server farm in Virginia.

[beat: the implications]

Think about what this means. Every smart home company — Amazon, Google, Apple, Samsung — has built its entire business model around being the middleman. The hub. The speaker. The app. The cloud service. Neuralink just made the middleman optional.

If your brain can talk directly to your lights, your thermostat, your locks — why do you need a plastic cylinder on your counter listening to your conversations?
