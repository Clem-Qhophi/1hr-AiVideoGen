"Here are the exact terms and conditions" does not get clicks the way "free house" does. First, the mechanism itself. What these programs actually are. Nearly all of them work the same way. A city or a nonprofit partner gives you a second loan, on top of your regular mortgage, specifically for your down payment or closing costs. That second loan is forgivable. Meaning if you live in the house for a set number of years, usually five, you never pay it back at all. Leave early, and you typically owe some or all of it, prorated for how long you actually stayed. It works a lot like a signing bonus that vests over time at a new job. Stay the full term, and it is genuinely yours. Leave halfway through, and you owe back whatever has not vested yet. City one. South Bend, Indiana. Per Community Homebuyers Corporation, the city's program partner, qualifying buyers get a forgivable second mortgage equal to twenty percent of the home's purchase price. Twenty percent. On a lot of homes, that is the entire conventional down payment, covered. Say you are buying a home at South Bend's own price cap. On a hundred seventy five thousand dollar house, twenty percent works out to thirty five thousand dollars, forgiven, as long as you stay. There are real limits. The home price cannot exceed one hundred seventy five thousand dollars. And here is the rule that trips people up. You cannot already have a signed purchase agreement when you apply. Sign first, and you are disqualified before you even start. That single rule quietly knocks out a lot of applicants who did everything else right. They found a house, got excited, signed the offer, and only found this program afterward. By then, it is too late. City two. Baltimore, Maryland. Per Live Baltimore's own program page, the "Buying Into Baltimore" incentive offers five thousand dollars toward your down payment and closing costs. It forgives completely over five years of living there. Here is the catch. It is not first-come, first-served. It is a lottery. Only twenty winners are selected each year, and you have to attend a specific city tour event just to enter. You also have to put in one thousand dollars of your own money. Your mortgage has to stay under the FHA limit, currently around seven hundred thirty one thousand dollars. And only fixed-rate mortgages qualify. No adjustable rates, no owner financing, no cash deals. City three. Newark, New Jersey. Per the city's own Live Newark program page, first-time buyers can get a twenty thousand dollar loan. Zero percent interest. Usable for closing costs and your down payment. It forgives at twenty percent a year. Stay five years, owe nothing. Per the city's own data, the average buyer using this program earned around fifty four thousand dollars a year. The average home they bought cost around two hundred thirty five thousand dollars. This is not a program built for luxury buyers. It is built for exactly the buyer most down payment content online ignores completely. Notice what all three have in common. None of them replace a mortgage. Every single one still requires you to qualify for, and close on, a real loan from a real private lender. The city money covers part of the down payment. It does not cover the loan itself. It also does not remove underwriting, which is the bank's own process of checking your income, your credit, and the house before saying yes. You still need decent credit. You still need provable income. You still need a house that passes inspection. The city program removes one obstacle, the cash up front, not every obstacle.
