Neuralink raised $650 million this year. None of it has gone toward getting their brain-implant vision device, Blindsight, into an actual human trial — even though the FDA fast-tracked it back in September 2024.

This video checks the primary source: Neuralink's own clinical trials page (live today) lists their motor and speech programs as "Active trial" with 21 real participants, while Blindsight still says "Upcoming trial." Sixteen months of official silence on the one program that was supposed to restore sight, while everything else got real updates. Named experts on record — Ione Fine (University of Washington) and Philip Troyk (Illinois Tech) — explain why vision is a genuinely harder problem than motor control or speech, and a vision scientist at Johns Hopkins has a blunt read on what Neuralink has actually shown so far.

The goal is real. The science is real. What hasn't happened yet is the human trial the headlines already claimed.
